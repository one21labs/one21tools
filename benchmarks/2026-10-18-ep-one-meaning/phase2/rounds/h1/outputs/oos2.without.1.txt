No, I don't agree. The two sprints are already spent whether you keep the cache or not. That's a sunk cost, and it shouldn't drive the decision. What matters is the cost from here on.

**Why the standard map likely wins:**
- **Maintenance:** Custom code needs its own tests, bug fixes, and onboarding time for new people. The standard library map has none of that.
- **Performance:** It's measurably faster in your own benchmarks.
- **Correctness:** The standard map has years of production use. A custom cache will have more edge cases, especially around concurrency, eviction, and memory growth.

**When keeping it could make sense:**
- It has features the standard map lacks, such as TTL, LRU eviction, size bounds, or metrics, and you need them. If so, compare it against a mature library that provides those features, not just the raw map.
- The benchmark doesn't match your real workload. Check access patterns, concurrency level, key and value sizes, and GC pressure. If the cache wins where it counts, such as lower tail latency or less memory, that's a real reason to keep it.
- You expect to optimize it soon, and you have a specific plan with evidence behind it.

**What I'd do:**
1. Check that the benchmark is fair: realistic workload, warmed up, and run enough times to be meaningful.
2. List the features you actually need.
3. If the standard map, or a maintained library built on it, covers those needs, switch to it.
4. Write down what you learned from the cache work. That is the part of the investment you can keep.

Two sprints is a real cost, but it's smaller than years of maintaining something slower than the alternative.
