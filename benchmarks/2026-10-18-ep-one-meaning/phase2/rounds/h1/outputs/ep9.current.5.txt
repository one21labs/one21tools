**Verdict: not ready to build.** The design has a correctness hole on the one hard requirement, and most of the 6 weeks goes to features nothing in the context justifies.

## Blocking: the design can send invoices twice

The requirement is that an invoice is never sent twice. The design breaks it in three places:

1. **The 5s timeout re-enqueues a job whose email may already be sent.** A timeout means you don't know the outcome. The provider may have accepted the request and answered slowly. Marking the job failed and retrying sends a second invoice, which is exactly the chargeback scenario.
2. **A crash between "provider accepted" and "mark done" causes the same duplicate.** The design has no lease or visibility timeout for a worker that dies mid-job. A job stuck in `running` is either never retried or retried blindly.
3. **Nothing stops the same invoice being enqueued twice.** The design has no dedupe key, so a double-click or a caller retry creates two jobs.

Fixes to put in the design before building:
- Add a unique `(type, dedupe_key)` constraint, for example `invoice:<invoice_id>`, so enqueue is idempotent.
- Send the dedupe key to the provider as an idempotency key. Check whether your provider supports one. If it doesn't, that changes the design.
- Give each job type a delivery semantic. Password resets and receipts can be at-least-once. Invoices need at-most-once or idempotent send. On timeout or an ambiguous crash, an invoice job goes to an `unknown` state. A worker resolves it by looking up the message at the provider, or a human resolves it. It is never blindly re-enqueued.
- Add a lease (`locked_until`) with a reaper, and record `sent_at` or the provider message ID in the same transaction that completes the job.

## Scope: most of the 6 weeks isn't justified

200 to 500 emails a day is about one every few minutes. Compare each item against that:

| Item | Assessment |
|---|---|
| In-house queue at all | Evaluate pg-boss, Graphile Worker, River or Oban (whichever fits your stack) first. They already provide SKIP LOCKED, backoff, and leases. State why they don't fit. |
| Plugin system with YAML manifests | No other team has asked for it. Cut it. A typed handler registry in code is enough. |
| 5 priority levels with weighted fair scheduling | Starvation isn't a risk at this volume. Use at most two lanes, or none. |
| React admin dashboard | A few SQL queries or an off-the-shelf board covers inspection and manual retry. Add a retry CLI command if needed. |
| 500ms polling | Harmless at this volume, but 5 to 10s polling or LISTEN/NOTIFY is enough. |

## Gaps in the checklist

- **Success criteria:** None are stated. Examples: zero duplicate invoices, a maximum delay for password resets, alerting when a job dead-letters.
- **Dead-letter and alerting:** After 5 failed attempts the design doesn't say what happens. Someone must be told, especially for invoices.
- **Retention and PII:** Payloads contain customer emails and invoice data. Define how long they are kept and whether they are redacted.
- **Provider failure modes:** The design doesn't distinguish 4xx (don't retry), 429 and 5xx (retry), and timeouts (ambiguous).
- **Verification:** Plan a test that kills the worker mid-send and one that makes the provider time out after accepting. Both must produce zero duplicate invoices.

## Recommendation

Rescope to a v1 of about 1 to 2 weeks:
- Postgres table with `dedupe_key` unique, lease, attempts, and `provider_message_id`.
- A worker with per-type delivery semantics.
- A dead-letter state with an alert.
- The two crash and timeout tests above.

Defer the plugin system, the priority scheduler and the dashboard until a second team or a real incident asks for them. First, decide whether an existing library covers this, and confirm whether your email provider supports idempotency keys.
