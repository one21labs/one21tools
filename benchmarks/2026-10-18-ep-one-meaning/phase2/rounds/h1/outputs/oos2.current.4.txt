No, I disagree. The two sprints are already spent whether you keep the cache or delete it, so they shouldn't count in the decision. What matters is what each option costs from here on.

**Keeping it:**
- It's slower than the standard library map.
- Your team has to maintain it, and the bugs in it are yours to fix.
- New engineers have to learn it.

**Switching to the standard map:**
- You get better performance now.
- You pay a small migration cost.
- Maintenance moves to code the language team already looks after.

Before you delete it, check these:

1. **Is the benchmark realistic?** Does it use your real key distribution, value sizes, read/write ratio and concurrency? Microbenchmarks can mislead, especially under contention.
2. **Does the cache do something the map doesn't?** If it handles eviction, TTLs, size bounds, memory limits or lock-free concurrent access, the plain map isn't a like-for-like replacement. In that case the question is whether you can get those features cheaply, for example from a mature library or by wrapping the standard map. Don't keep a slower implementation just because it exists.
3. **Is the performance gap important?** If the cache isn't on a hot path, the difference may not matter. Even then, ongoing maintenance of custom code with no performance advantage is still a cost.
4. **Does it have a plausible path to being faster?** If you know why it's slow and the fix is cheap, give it one short, time-boxed attempt. Don't sink another sprint into it.

If the answers are "realistic benchmark, no unique features, it matters," replace it. Before you delete it, write up what you learned, including why it was slower and what the benchmarks showed. That's the part of the two sprints you can still use.
