# Python 3.8 → 3.12 Sprint Plan (Mon Oct 19 – Fri Oct 30)

## Decisions to get from the director first

I kept the freeze and the two-week window. I changed two things, and the plan is built on those changes:

1. **Deploy in waves on Tue–Thu of week 2, not one big bang on Friday.**
   - Your on-call rollback requirement is hard to meet with a big bang. If the 40 services flip together, a bad dependency interaction (payments plus an upstream caller, say) forces you to roll back everything or debug live over the weekend.
   - A Friday deploy also leaves on-call with the weekend as the soak period.
   - Everyone still upgrades inside the sprint, and Friday becomes buffer and verification.
   - If the answer is still "Friday big bang," see the fallback at the bottom.
2. **Add a per-service Python version to the Helm chart.**
   - The base image is currently one pin in `values.yaml`, so changing it flips everything at once.
   - Add a single `pythonVersion` default plus an optional per-service override. The image tag is derived from that one value, so there is no duplicated literal and no per-service image strings.
   - Rollback for a service is then one value flipped back, or `helm rollback`.
   - This is Day 1 work and it blocks everything else.

## Open gaps (answer by Tuesday)

- **Payments webhook receiver, no tests:** who owns it, and is there recorded production traffic (payloads and signatures) to build tests from?
- **libgeo-py:** who owns it, is there a build pipeline for cp312 wheels, and does it use any C API removed between 3.8 and 3.12? This is the critical path.
- **Data compatibility:** do any services share pickles, caches or queues across processes? Mixed 3.8 and 3.12 pods must coexist during the waves.
- **Staging parity:** does staging run the same chart with real dependencies? Without it, "tests pass" is the only gate.
- **Success criteria:** error rate, p99 latency and payment success rate stay within baseline for 24h after each wave.

## Day-by-day

**Week 1: upgrade and prove it**

- **Mon:**
  - F: chart override merged with default still 3.8 (no behavior change), and 3.12 image built.
  - Everyone else: an hour of triage, then start. Each team runs its service's CI on 3.12 to get a baseline red/green list.
  - C: confirm libgeo-py ownership and start the wheel build.
  - E: pull payload samples.
- **Tue:**
  - Gaps above answered.
  - A, B and D in full swing.
  - Rollback drill on one low-risk service in staging: flip to 3.12, then back.
- **Wed:**
  - First 3.12 wheels for libgeo-py in the index, or escalate immediately if the build is hard.
  - E: characterization tests green on 3.8.
  - A: aim for most services green in CI.
- **Thu:**
  - Remaining red services triaged, with a hard list of stragglers.
  - E: tests green on 3.12.
  - Every service that is green deploys to staging on 3.12.
- **Fri:**
  - All 40 on 3.12 in staging, soaking over the weekend.
  - **Gate 1:** the list of services not ready is reviewed, with a decision on each.

**Week 2: deploy in waves**

- **Mon:** Staging soak review. Fix findings. Rollback runbook finalized. On-call briefed.
- **Tue, Wave 1 (about 10 low-risk services):** deploy in the morning, watch for 24h. Flip back immediately on any regression.
- **Wed, Wave 2 (about 20 services, including the asyncio and distutils ones):** same procedure.
- **Thu, Wave 3 (libgeo-py services, then the payments webhook receiver last):** payments gets a canary (one pod or 5–10% of traffic) held for at least 2 hours before full rollout. Don't flip it unattended.
- **Fri, buffer:** no deploys except fixes. Verify metrics, close out stragglers, and run a short retro. The freeze lifts when Wave 3 is stable, not before.

## Rollback

- **Mechanism:** each service's `pythonVersion` override reverts to 3.8, or `helm rollback`. Rollback takes minutes and needs no rebuild.
- **Kept alive for rollback:** the 3.8 image tag, the cp38 wheels, and the old lockfiles on a tagged branch. Don't delete any of them until at least a week after Wave 3.
- **Drill:** run the rollback once per wave type (plain service, libgeo service, payments) in staging before the wave.
- **Trigger:** on-call rolls back without needing approval if error rate or latency exceeds the agreed thresholds. Debugging happens after the rollback.
- **Mixed versions:** pods on 3.8 and 3.12 run side by side during waves. Confirm there are no cross-process pickles or caches (see gaps).

## Go/no-go gates

- **Gate 1 (Fri wk1):** the service is green on 3.12 in CI and staging, and has a rollback entry in the runbook. A service that misses this gate does not go in its wave. It moves to a follow-up and stays on 3.8 via the override.
- **Gate 2 (before payments):** webhook characterization tests green on 3.12, canary plan agreed with the payments owner, and rollback tested.

## Main risks

1. **libgeo-py wheels** (critical path). If cp312 builds slip past Wednesday, those two services ship in a later sprint on 3.8. The per-service override makes this safe.
2. **Payments with no tests.** Characterization tests plus a canary are the minimum. Don't ship it on "it started up."
3. **Transitive dependency surprises** in the 31, concentrated in the 60–70% coverage services. Budget the most triage time for them.
4. **A 14-day freeze with all hands on one change** leaves little slack. Protect the Friday buffer.

## Fallback if the director insists on a Friday big bang

- Deploy as one coordinated change, but only after Waves 1–3 have all been proven in staging on the exact chart.
- Keep per-service overrides so the rollback is a values revert, not a rebuild.
- Do the payments canary first and keep the whole team on call through the weekend.
- Document in writing that rollback is all-or-nothing in this mode, and who accepted that risk.
