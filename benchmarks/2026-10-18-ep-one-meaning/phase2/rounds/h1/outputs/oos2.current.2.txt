No, I don't agree. The two sprints are a sunk cost: you can't get them back whether you keep the cache or delete it. The only question is which option is cheaper and better from here.

Your benchmarks say the custom cache is slower than the standard library map. Keeping it means you maintain more code, carry more bug risk, and onboard people onto a custom component, all for worse performance. The standard map costs you none of that.

**Check these before you delete it:**
- **Is the benchmark realistic?** Check that it uses your real access patterns, key sizes, concurrency level, and hit/miss ratio. A microbenchmark can mislead.
- **Does the cache provide features the map lacks?** Eviction (LRU/TTL), size bounds, and thread-safe access are the usual ones. If you need those, compare against a maintained library such as a well-tested LRU or TTL cache, not a bare map.
- **Is raw speed what you're optimizing for?** If the cache wins on memory footprint, GC pressure, or tail latency, that could justify keeping it. Your benchmarks would need to show it.

If it's slower and adds no features you need, replace it with the standard map or a maintained library and delete the custom code.

The two sprints weren't wasted. You now have evidence about what doesn't help, and you know your access patterns better. If you want, I can help write a more representative benchmark or a short decision note for the team.
