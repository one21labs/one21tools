No, I don't agree. The two sprints are a sunk cost. They're gone whether you keep the cache or delete it, so they shouldn't count in the decision.

**What should count:**
- **Cost of keeping it:** You'd carry code you have to maintain, test, and debug, and it's slower than `map`. That's a cost with no benefit.
- **Cost of switching:** Swapping in the standard library map is probably a small change, especially if the cache sits behind an interface.
- **Benefit of the custom cache:** Only count this if it does something `map` can't. Examples are eviction (LRU or TTL), a memory bound, or better behavior under concurrent access. If so, the question becomes whether you need those features, not how much effort went in.

**Before you delete it, check:**
1. Are the benchmarks realistic? Look at the access patterns, key distribution, concurrency level, and working-set size. A plain map can win on microbenchmarks and lose in production.
2. Does the benchmark compare equal features? A bare map has no eviction and no thread safety. If you need those, compare against a map plus a mutex, or an off-the-shelf library such as an LRU package, not a bare map.
3. Is speed the actual goal? If the cache exists for a different reason, such as bounded memory or less GC pressure, raw lookup speed may not be the right measure.

**My recommendation:** If the benchmarks are fair and you don't need features beyond `map`, replace the custom cache with the standard library. If you need extra features, evaluate a maintained library before keeping custom code. You can still keep what the two sprints taught you, such as the benchmark harness and your understanding of the access patterns.

Keep the cache only if it's the best option today. "We already built it" isn't a reason to keep it.
