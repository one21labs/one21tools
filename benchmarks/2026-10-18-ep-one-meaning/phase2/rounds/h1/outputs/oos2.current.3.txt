No, I don't agree. The two sprints are a sunk cost, and they shouldn't drive the decision. The question is which option is cheaper and better from here on.

The custom cache is slower, and you'd also own its bugs, tests, and maintenance. The standard library map is faster and costs you nothing to maintain. Keeping the cache means paying twice: once for the sprints already spent, and again every time someone has to touch it.

Before you delete it, check three things:

1. **Is the benchmark representative?** Check that it uses your real key distribution, value sizes, read/write ratio, and concurrency. A microbenchmark on a single thread can mislead if production is heavily concurrent.
2. **Does the cache do something the map doesn't?** Eviction (LRU/LFU), TTLs, size bounds, and memory limits aren't in a plain map. If you need those, you may still need a cache, but a maintained library is usually a better choice than your own.
3. **Does speed matter on your critical path?** If cache operations are a negligible part of request latency, correctness and simplicity matter more than raw speed.

If those checks don't change the picture, replace it with the standard map (or a proven library if you need eviction) and delete the custom code. Git history keeps it if you ever want it back.

The two sprints weren't wasted, either. You now have benchmark evidence and a clearer idea of your requirements. A short note on what you learned will be worth more than the code.
