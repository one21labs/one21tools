No, I don't agree. The two sprints are already spent whether you keep the cache or not. The only question that matters now is which option is cheaper and better from here on.

If the cache is slower than the standard map, keeping it means you:

- **Pay a performance penalty** on every operation, with nothing in return.
- **Own more code**: bugs, concurrency edge cases, and onboarding cost for new people.
- **Keep paying maintenance** indefinitely, for something the stdlib gives you for free.

Before you drop it, check a few things:

1. **Is the benchmark representative?** Microbenchmarks often miss the real workload: key distribution, value sizes, concurrency level, and memory and GC pressure. If your cache wins under realistic contention, or has a lower memory footprint, the benchmark may be measuring the wrong thing.
2. **Does it provide features the plain map doesn't?** Eviction (LRU/LFU), TTLs, size bounds, or metrics. A plain map has none of these, so the comparison may not be apples to apples. If you need those features, compare against a mature library (for example Caffeine, Ristretto, or `lru-cache`, depending on your language) rather than a raw map.
3. **Is the gap big enough to matter?** If the difference is a few percent and nowhere near your bottleneck, performance may not decide this. The maintenance burden still counts against it.

**My recommendation:** if the cache has no features you need and no realistic workload where it wins, replace it with the stdlib map (or a well-tested library if you need eviction). Treat the two sprints as a lesson learned. A short write-up of what you tried and why it didn't pay off is a good way to get some value from them.
